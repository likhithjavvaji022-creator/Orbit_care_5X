ORBITCARE 5X - WHAT THIS PROJECT IS, IN SIMPLE WORDS
====================================================

1. THE BIG IDEA
---------------
Astronauts in space face 5 big dangers (NASA calls them "hazards"):

  1. Space radiation       - invisible energy from the Sun and stars that can harm the body
  2. Distance from Earth   - far away, messages to doctors on Earth take a long time
  3. Low gravity           - muscles and bones weaken without exercise
  4. Closed air system     - the air inside the spaceship must be kept safe (oxygen, CO2)
  5. Isolation and stress  - poor sleep, tiredness and stress

ORBITCARE 5X is a website that watches all 5 dangers at once and gives the
astronaut a simple "report card" grade, like in school: A+, A, B, C, D or F.


2. HOW THE GRADING WORKS
------------------------
- Each of the 5 dangers gets its own score from 0 to 100.
  Everyone starts at 100 (perfect) on Day 1. Points are taken away when
  something looks bad (for example: CO2 in the air too high, slept less
  than 6 hours, too much radiation).

- Each score also gets a colour:
    GREEN  = fine (75 or more)
    YELLOW = getting worse (35 to 74)
    RED    = serious (below 35)

- The 5 scores are combined into ONE overall "Total Mission Grade".
  Some dangers count more than others:
    Radiation 25%, Air system 25%, Stress/sleep 20%,
    Gravity 15%, Distance 15%

- Two safety rules:
    a) If ANY one danger is RED, the total grade can't be better than D,
       even if everything else is perfect. (One serious problem matters.)
    b) If 3 or more dangers are YELLOW or RED at the same time,
       10 extra points are taken off. (Many problems together are worse.)

- Letter grades: 95+ = A+, 90+ = A, 75+ = B, 55+ = C, 35+ = D, below 35 = F.


3. WHAT YOU SEE ON THE WEBSITE (12 PAGES)
-----------------------------------------
 1. Mission Control     - the main screen: 5 coloured tiles, the big total grade,
                          a chart of how the grade changes day by day, and a
                          button to upload a data file.
 2. Space Weather       - REAL live data from NOAA (the US space weather agency)
                          about solar activity. If it can't connect, it says so
                          and shows a "Retry" button - it never makes numbers up.
 3. Radiation Guard     - radiation details.
 4. Crew Health /
    Mental Health Guard - sleep, heart-rate, tiredness and stress.
 5. Gravity Guard       - exercise and balance.
 6. Life Support Guard  - oxygen, CO2, air pressure, temperature, humidity.
 7. Earth Distance Guard- message delay to Earth, plus a simple step-by-step
                          checklist helper (it never suggests medicines).
 8. AI Risk Analysis    - explains in plain words WHY each score dropped.
 9. Flight Surgeon HUD  - a quick one-glance summary for the space doctor.
10. Alerts              - warnings when something turns yellow or red.
11. Mission Simulator   - "what if" sliders: move them and watch the grade change.
12. Reports & Settings  - download data, change the grading weights, reset the demo.

Not finished: the "NASA Research" page. It is clearly marked "not connected"
instead of showing anything fake.


4. WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM
----------------------------
- There are no real astronauts here. All crew data is SIMULATED (made up by a
  computer program) for a fictional astronaut called "SIM-A1", and every
  screen labels it SIMULATED.
- The sample file (data/sample/workflow_a_31_scenarios.csv) has 32 days:
  Day 1 is perfect, then each day turns on a different mix of the 5 dangers
  (there are 31 possible mixes). Uploading it shows the grade sliding from
  A+ (about 98.5) down to D (39.6).
- "Live Simulation Stream" button: adds a new pretend day every 4 seconds.
- Space weather is the only live, real-world data.


5. WHAT HAPPENS BEHIND THE SCENES (7 STEPS)
-------------------------------------------
When data comes in, it goes through 7 steps, like a factory line:
  1. Receive the readings
  2. Check them (throw out impossible values, duplicates, future dates)
  3. Learn what is "normal" for that astronaut
  4. Spot unusual changes
  5. Calculate the 5 scores and the total grade
  6. Prepare the doctor's summary screen
  7. Create warnings (without repeating the same warning twice a day)


6. IMPORTANT HONESTY NOTES
--------------------------
- This is NOT a medical device. It does not diagnose anyone. The grades are an
  illustrative "screening" score for a hackathon demo.
- The point deductions are example numbers chosen for the demo, not scientific
  medical values.
- It does not predict solar storms.


7. HOW IT IS BUILT (FOR THE CURIOUS)
------------------------------------
- Back end (the "engine", runs on your computer): Python, FastAPI, SQLite database.
- Front end (what you see in the browser): plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and
  Chart.js for charts.
- 36 automatic tests check the grading maths and the website's data functions.
  All 36 passed on the last run.
- There is also a single-file version: dist/orbitcare5x.html


8. HOW TO RUN IT
----------------
Open a terminal in this folder and type:

    pip install -r requirements.txt
    python -m uvicorn backend.app.main:app --port 8000

Then open http://localhost:8000 in your web browser.
On Mission Control, upload: data/sample/workflow_a_31_scenarios.csv

To run the automatic tests:

    python -m pytest backend/tests -q
